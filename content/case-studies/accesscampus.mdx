## The problem

Most map apps assume you can walk any route they suggest. They rarely say whether a route has stairs, a ramp, a working lift or a clear path.

On campus, that information is scattered across building signs and staff who have to be asked. Planning a trip gets harder for the people who most need to plan.

The audience is people with limited mobility on campus: students and university staff.

### What the research showed

I grouped everything into six themes.

1. **Facility information is missing or unclear.** Ramps, lifts, handrails, benches and charging points.
2. **The path can't be predicted.** Slippery tile in the rain, rough gravel, steep ramps, narrow paths, steps before the lift.
3. **Information changes and nobody updates it.** Broken lifts are known only by word of mouth.
4. **Trips cost more time and energy.** One person leaves 20–30 minutes early for every trip.
5. **People depend on others for information.** They ask staff, colleagues or whoever is nearby.
6. **The app itself has to be accessible.** Small text and complex apps shut people out.

Themes 1–3 appeared in nearly every persona, so they became the core problem. Themes 4–5 are what those problems cause. Theme 6 applies to every feature.

## Defining the problem

<Callout>
People with limited mobility on campus need to know in advance which route will really get them there with their limits that day, because ramp, lift and path information is incomplete, out of date and scattered. So they depend on others, and spend more time and energy than everyone else.
</Callout>

I wrote five How Might We questions and took two into the MVP:

- **In the MVP:** how might we help people know which route is really accessible for them before they leave?
- **In the MVP:** how might we keep information about broken lifts and closed paths up to date?
- How might we make path conditions readable at a glance?
- How might we offer a backup when the plan fails partway?
- How might we make reporting and confirming easy, with no long typing?

## Choosing what to build

I sorted the ideas by impact and effort. These are my own estimates.

<Grid>
<Callout>
**Quick wins: in the MVP**

- Needs profile that filters routes
- "Accessible" or "Has limits" labels
- Icons for ramps, lifts, stairs and benches
- Lift status with last-updated time
- Report a problem in 1–2 taps
- Other users confirm reports
</Callout>
<Callout>
**Big bets: next phase**

- Travel time at each person's pace
- Step-free entrances and drop-off points
- Alerts on saved routes
- Data from the campus facilities team
</Callout>
<Callout>
**Fill-ins: if there's time**

- Photos of key spots
</Callout>
<Callout>
**Not doing**

- AR navigation
- IoT sensors in lifts
</Callout>
</Grid>

### Design principles from theme 6

- Large, resizable text
- Symbols and colors that read instantly
- Reporting by tap or voice, with no typing
- Mobile first

The flow: set your needs once, get routes labeled for you, see lift and path status with update times, and report or confirm a problem in 1–2 taps. Where the information isn't enough, the app says so instead of guessing, and it can't promise more safety than the data supports.
