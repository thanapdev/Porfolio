## My role

We were a team of 2. I explored the problem and the audience, drew the wireframes and annotations, and made about 70% of the Figma UI and interactions. My teammate interviewed the target users, gathered the published research and built the journey map. We defined the features together.

## The problem

Some people get so unhappy with a roommate that they want to live alone, but the economy forces them to keep sharing the rent. Much of that comes from one gap: nobody knows how the other person lives until it is too late.

Trash left around, a room nobody cleans, belongings used without asking, and outsiders brought in without warning all came up again and again.

Students are the main audience. Many move away from home for university with no close friends nearby, so they need someone to share with, and most want to split the rent. Working people are the second group. They screen carefully, and would rather pay more to live alone than come home from work to roommate problems.

### What published sources say

- A column in *The Vanderbilt Hustler* (Anisa Juma, 2025) calls a bad roommate a "personal hell" that can ruin the college experience.
- A paper in the *Journal of Public Health in the Deep South* links negative roommate relationships to students' health and wellbeing, with poorer rest and more stress.
- A document from the Chiang Mai Provincial Public Health Office stresses preventing conflict early, through shared agreements and good communication.

So finding a compatible roommate and agreeing on rules early affects health as well as comfort.

## Defining the problem

<Callout>
**Functional pain:** finding a roommate is spread across X, Facebook and Line groups. Status is never live, so people message someone only to hear the spot is gone.

**Emotional pain:** it is hard to raise rules with a stranger, or to speak up about late gaming, loud calls or a partner staying over. Stress builds in the place that should be for rest.

**Trust pain:** people worry about being stood up, about not getting along, and about whether the other person will pay the rent on time.
</Callout>

## The idea

**A good roommate is someone with a similar lifestyle.** RoomieMatch matches on room needs, then uses each person's lifestyle to work out a compatibility percentage. Even two people who love loud late-night gaming can be a great match.

It also stays useful after the match, through finding someone, getting to know them, agreeing to room together and moving in.

### Key features

- **Two roles.** Room Owners and Room Seekers each see the people relevant to them, so owners do not see other owners.
- **Search by train line.** Pick a BTS, MRT or ARL station and see the real distance, such as 500 m from the station.
- **Swipe with data.** The compatibility score sits on the profile card.
- **Making Contact.** Sending a formal request and being accepted hides the profile from others, and "Contact in Progress" stops overlapping conversations.
- **After the match.** The status changes to Confirm Roommate once the lease is agreed. Our schedule is a shared calendar, and both people can edit Our agreement.
